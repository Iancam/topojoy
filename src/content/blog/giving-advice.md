# Giving Advice

A gift should be given wherever the heart feels inspired, and if you're interested in getting good results from generosity in terms of future happiness, it helps to treat it as a skill. So, think of skills you've developed in the past, like writing or sports or playing an instrument. It takes time and effort to develop them. The big pattern for learning is to commit to some action you think will be good and then reflect on the results. It's the same with giving: you commit to giving a couple of gifts and then reflect. Then you do it all over again. Does this giving thing really make me happy? Are there ways that I can improve?

It helps to have some guidance in the reflection process. The advice I'm using comes from Buddhism, but you don't have to be Buddhist to find it useful. It breaks gifts down into 4 parts: **Motivation, attitude, recipient, and gift**.

Why are you giving? This is **motivation,** and the Buddha describes gradations of effectiveness, starting with the desire to store up wealth for a future life, through to the highest, which is simply the thought that the act of giving is an ornament and support for the heart here and now. One motivation that works is the thought that you're developing the useful mental quality of generosity.

From [a book by Thanissaro Bhikkhu](https://www.dhammatalks.org/books/uncollected/Happiness_asSkill.html):

> In terms of the **attitude,** the Buddha recommends that you give attentively, with the conviction that something good will come of the gift, with empathy for the person who’s receiving the gift, and not with the sense that you’re simply throwing it away. In other words, the way you talk to yourself about the meaning and importance of what you’re doing while giving a gift plays a large role in how much happiness you derive from the act.

Giving goes to the heart when you spend some time thinking about the gift: what would really help this person? Rather than just going through the motions, think of what you can give of yourself–your observations, your creativity–that would help the recipient.

As for the **recipient,** you want to give to someone to whom, when you later reflect on the gift and its consequences, you’ll be happy you gave. The research in Give and Take, a book focused on "giver mindset" in the context of worldly success, agrees: givers giving to givers tend to reap long-term rewards, while givers giving to takers can be taken advantage of.

The **gift** itself should be appropriate to time and place and shouldn't hurt you or anyone else. So don’t give so much that you harm yourself financially, don’t steal the gift to give it away, and don’t give a gift that will place undue burdens on the recipient.

Finally, if you dig the Buddha's strategies for happiness, he is quick to note that the happiness from an especially skillful gift is no match at all for the goodness that comes from observing the five precepts. That goodness, in turn, is no match for developing a heart of goodwill.
